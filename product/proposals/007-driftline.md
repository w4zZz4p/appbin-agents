# 007: Driftline (concept)
- Author: PO-Tech
- Status: accepted (merged into decision 001 as base)
- Focus: tech & performance

**Pitch:** Steer a paper boat down an endless river with one thumb or one mouse button. Grab lanterns, dodge rocks, and the current gets faster and narrower until you crash.

**Core fantasy:** Calm, then suddenly tense. You are a small, fragile thing on a big, indifferent current, and you are very good at threading gaps.

## 30-second core loop
1. Hold/tap left or right half of the screen (or A/D keys) to nudge the boat across the river.
2. Lanterns in the channel add score and a few seconds of "light" (the screen widens its view). Rocks and banks end the run.
3. Every ~10 seconds the river narrows and speeds up. Run ends after 60–150 s. "One more run" restart is under 1 s.

## Player problem / opportunity
- Players on portals want a game they understand in 3 seconds and can drop in and out of. Current portal arcade games often have heavy load screens or require a tutorial.
- Opportunity: a skill game whose entire content is one canvas, one input, and a procedural river, so the first load is tiny and runs smoothly on a 3-year-old phone.

## Proposed feature (first version)
- Single screen, no menus before first run. Title is the first run itself (first death shows "Tap to drift again").
- Procedural river from a seed: the river is generated by a small deterministic function, so a **daily seed** gives everyone the same course. Shareable as "Beat my 84 on seed #412."
- Best score and last 5 scores in localStorage. Optional, never required.
- Rewarded ad offered once per death as "continue from last lantern" (one per run). No interstitial during a run.
- Cosmetic boat skins later (no stat changes).

## Platform notes
- **Mobile:** whole-screen tap halves; portrait-first, rotate allowed. Target 60 FPS with ~200 draw calls: plain Canvas 2D with pre-rendered sprite atlas.
- **Desktop:** mouse click halves or arrow/A-D keys. Same canvas scales to window.
- **Download:** estimated < 250 KB gzipped total (game code ~40 KB, atlas as one PNG ~60 KB, audio as 3 short Opus clips ~80 KB; no framework). Audio is optional at load and can start after first input.
- **Storage:** localStorage for scores only; game works fully if storage is wiped.
- **Server:** none for v1. Daily seed is computed client-side from the date. Leaderboard is a later, optional static-file-plus-serverless add-on.

## Value & metrics
- Time to first fun: target < 10 s (first tap moves the boat, first lantern within 5 s).
- Average session: 4–8 min (about 8–15 runs).
- D1 retention: target 25–30% if the daily seed brings people back.
- Share rate: seed-score challenge links; target 3–5% of sessions produce a share.
- ARPDAU: low but positive from rewarded ads only (assumption: ~$0.01–0.03 per DAU).

## Effort & risks
- Size: **S–M** (one developer, 3–5 weeks to a polished v1 including audio and balance).
- Art/audio: minimal vector or pixel art; 3 SFX + 1 loop.
- Risks: balance tuning of river generator is the real work; a monotonic difficulty curve can feel unfair if the generator makes unwinnable bends (mitigate by generator guaranteeing a passable lane). Rewarded-ad continue must not be shown more than once per run to avoid fatigue.
- RICE (rough): Reach 60% of visitors, Impact 2 (fun + retention), Confidence 70%, Effort 3 person-weeks → score ≈ 0.6 × 2 × 0.7 / 3 × 1000 ≈ 280.

## Smallest playable test
- Two-day prototype: one canvas, procedural river, one-thumb steering, score only. Put it on a static page, measure load size and FPS on a low-end Android (e.g. a 2019-era device in Chrome) and run 20 players on a link to see whether they say "one more" after the 3rd death.
- Kill criteria: median session under 2 min or fewer than 40% of testers voluntarily start a second run.
